# Quick-Commerce-Marketing-Research

## Consumer Behaviour and Marketing Research on Quick-Commerce Platforms in India

### Project Overview
This project examines consumer behaviour, preferences, adoption patterns, purchasing habits, and satisfaction associated with quick-commerce platforms in India. The study analyses respondent data to understand the factors influencing consumer choices, including delivery speed, discounts, pricing, product availability, and application usability.

The research combines descriptive statistical analysis with hypothesis testing using IBM SPSS Statistics.

## Research Objectives
- Examine consumer awareness and adoption of quick-commerce services.
- Identify commonly used quick-commerce platforms.
- Analyse consumer purchasing frequency, spending patterns, and product preferences.
- Investigate factors influencing consumer choices.
- Evaluate consumer satisfaction with quick-commerce services.
- Examine the relationship between consumer satisfaction and future-use intention.
- Test associations between selected consumer characteristics and purchasing behaviour.

## Platforms Studied
- Blinkit
- Zepto
- Swiggy Instamart
- BigBasket BBNow
- Amazon Rapid
- Flipkart Minutes

## Dataset
- Sample size: 150 respondent records
- User records: 105
- Non-user records: 45
- Dataset format: Excel
- Analysis software: IBM SPSS Statistics

The analysis uses a sample of 150 respondent records. Variable-specific analyses use available valid responses, depending on missing data.

## Research Methodology

### Descriptive Analysis
The study uses frequency distributions and percentages to examine:

- Demographic characteristics
- Awareness and adoption
- Platform usage
- Order frequency
- First-trial reasons
- Spontaneous purchasing frequency
- Order spending
- Product categories
- Factors influencing platform selection
- Satisfaction levels
- Future-use intention

### Inferential Statistical Analysis

The following statistical techniques were applied using SPSS:

- **Chi-square test of independence:** To examine associations between selected categorical variables.
- **Spearman's rank correlation:** To evaluate the relationship between overall satisfaction and future-use intention.

## Hypothesis Testing

| Relationship Examined | Statistical Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Age group and quick-commerce adoption | Chi-square | χ² = 6.036, p = 0.303 |
| Discount/coupon selection and order spending | Chi-square | χ² = 2.116, p = 0.909 |
| Delivery speed selection and importance of fast delivery | Chi-square | χ² = 4.341, p = 0.362 |
| Discount/coupon selection and spontaneous purchasing frequency | Chi-square | χ² = 6.272, p = 0.180 |
| Overall satisfaction and future-use intention | Spearman correlation | ρ = 0.371, p < 0.001 |

The first four tests did not show statistically significant associations at the 5% significance level. The Spearman correlation showed a statistically significant positive relationship between overall satisfaction and future-use intention.

Some Chi-square analyses had low expected cell frequencies; these results should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

## Key Findings

- **90%** of respondents reported awareness of quick-commerce services.
- **70%** reported having ordered through a quick-commerce platform.
- Blinkit and Zepto were the most frequently selected platforms, each selected by 40% of respondents.
- Delivery speed was the most frequently selected influencing factor (50.7%).
- Faster delivery and convenience were the leading reasons for trying quick-commerce services.
- Snacks and beverages were the most frequently reported product category.
- Overall satisfaction was predominantly positive among valid responses.
- Satisfaction showed a significant positive association with future-use intention.

*Platform and influencing-factor percentages are based on respondent selections. Multiple responses were permitted.*

## Research Scope

The research focuses on consumer preferences and behavioural patterns associated with quick-commerce services in India. It examines adoption, platform selection, purchasing behaviour, influencing factors, satisfaction, and future-use intention.

The findings are limited by sample size, respondent representation, available data, and missing responses in certain analyses. The results should not be interpreted as representing the entire Indian consumer population.
